More about us

We are Team Divine Intervention

We build BOB, a small rescue robot that goes into collapsed buildings first, so that people trapped inside are found sooner and rescuers do not have to risk themselves to look.

Our name

Why "Divine Intervention"?

When a building comes down, the people under it are waiting for something to change. Every minute they hope that someone, somehow, finds them. People call that kind of rescue a miracle.

We do not believe in waiting for miracles. We believe in building them. Divine Intervention is the name we gave ourselves because we are the ones stepping into danger on someone's behalf, using engineering instead of luck.

BOB is how we do it. It crawls into the places that are too small, too hot or too unstable for a person, and it does the looking, so a human can still make every decision. To someone trapped in the dark, that arrival will feel like a miracle. To us, it is a very well-built robot.

A rescue that arrives in time can feel like a miracle. We want to make it a method.

Our purpose

Go in first, so people do not have to

After a collapse, the first hours matter most, and they are also the most dangerous for rescuers. Our purpose is to shorten the time it takes to find a survivor without adding another person to the danger.

  • Find people sooner

    BOB drives into gaps and sends back live video. Pip, its small scouting drone, looks from above and can see heat through smoke and dark.

  • Keep rescuers safer

    Unstable rubble, smoke and tight spaces put rescuers at risk before they find anyone. A robot can check first and tell them where it is safe to go.

  • Keep a human in control

    An operator drives BOB the whole time. The AI only highlights what looks like a person on the video. It never decides who gets help.

How it helps

From collapse to contact

  1. Drive in. The operator steers BOB across rough ground toward the collapse.
  2. Launch Pip. Pip leaves its bay and scouts from above.
  3. Find heat. Pip's thermal camera picks out human warmth through smoke and darkness.
  4. Mark what matters. An AI overlay highlights moving, warm or human-shaped objects for the operator to check.
  5. Deliver aid. BOB can drop a radio, first aid or medicine through its trapdoor to someone who is still waiting.
