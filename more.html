More about us

We are Team Divine Intervention

We build BOB, a small rescue robot that goes into dangerous places first, so that people in trouble are found sooner and rescuers do not have to risk themselves just to look.

Our name

Why "Divine Intervention"?

When disaster strikes, whether a building collapses, a fire spreads or the water rises, the people caught in it are waiting for something to change. Every minute they hope that someone, somehow, finds them. People call that kind of rescue a miracle.

We do not believe in waiting for miracles. We believe in building them. Divine Intervention is the name we gave ourselves because we are the ones stepping into danger on someone's behalf, using engineering instead of luck.

BOB is how we do it. It goes into the places that are too small, too hot or too unstable for a person, and it does the looking, so a human can still make every decision. To someone waiting in the dark, that arrival will feel like a miracle. To us, it is a very well-built robot.

A rescue that arrives in time can feel like a miracle. We want to make it a method.

Our purpose

Go in first, so people do not have to

In any emergency, the first hours matter most, and they are also the most dangerous for the people who come to help. Our purpose is to shorten the time it takes to find someone, without adding another person to the danger.

  • Find people sooner

    BOB drives into gaps and sends back live video. Pip, its small scouting drone, is designed to look from above and spot body heat through smoke and darkness.

  • Keep rescuers safer

    Unstable ground, smoke, water and tight spaces put rescuers at risk before they find anyone. A robot can check first and show them what is safe.

  • Keep a human in control

    An operator drives BOB the whole time. The AI only highlights what looks like a person on the video. It never decides who gets help.

Where it helps

Anywhere a person should not go first

BOB is not built for one kind of disaster. It is a small, tough, remote-controlled eyes-and-ears for any place that is too dangerous, too tight or too dark to enter blind.

  • Collapsed buildings
  • Earthquakes
  • Fires and smoke
  • Floods
  • Tunnels and mines
  • Industrial accidents
  • Gas leaks and hazardous spaces
  • Search in the dark

You can watch BOB and Pip work through four of these in the simulator on the scenarios page.

How it works

From call-out to contact

  1. Drive in. The operator steers BOB across rough ground toward the danger zone.
  2. Launch Pip. Pip leaves its bay on BOB and scouts from above.
  3. Find heat. Pip's thermal camera is designed to pick out human warmth through smoke and darkness.
  4. Mark what matters. An AI overlay highlights moving, warm or human-shaped objects for the operator to check.
  5. Deliver aid. BOB can drop a radio, first aid or medicine through its trapdoor to someone who is still waiting.

Some of this is built and some is our plan. Our slides show what BOB is today and where it is going.

For judges and visitors

Try the live camera feed

BOB's camera is private and opens with passcodes. There are three channels, and each channel has one passcode for the phone that rides on a robot and one for the laptop that watches it. Use the two from the same channel. The guide has every step.

Channel passcodes

Please keep these to yourself.

The passcodes are not published here yet. Write to bob@jrofficial.org and we will send them over.

Phone: open the camera page, enter the phone passcode and press Start camera. Laptop: open the control room, enter the laptop passcode and press Connect.
